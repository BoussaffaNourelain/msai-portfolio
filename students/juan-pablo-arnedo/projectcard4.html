Capstone: Local AI for Home Assistant

A home AI system that thinks, listens, watches, and acts — entirely on local hardware, with no data leaving the house.

The idea

The goal is to build an AI assistant for the home that works like the cloud assistants people already know — you can speak to it, it understands what you want, and it makes things happen — but with one critical difference: every piece of data stays inside the house.

No voice recording sent to a server. No camera image uploaded to the cloud. No conversation logged by a third party. The reasoning happens on local hardware, the cameras are processed locally, and the home automation runs on a local network. The system works even when the internet is down.

Why local matters

Cloud AI services are powerful, but every word you say and every image you share leaves your home and goes to a server you do not control. For a home assistant — where cameras see your rooms, microphones hear your conversations, and the system knows exactly when you are home — that is a real privacy trade-off, not a theoretical one.

Running the model locally means your home's data stays your home's data.

How it works — the components

Language model

Local LLM via Ollama

An open-weight language model (such as Llama or Mistral) runs on local hardware through Ollama. It handles natural-language understanding and task planning without needing an internet connection. This is the brain of the system.

Home automation

Home Assistant

The open-source home automation platform that controls lights, locks, thermostats, appliances, and any other connected device. The AI connects to it through its local API and can read sensor states and trigger actions.

Camera analysis

Frigate

An open-source NVR (network video recorder) with built-in AI object detection. Frigate processes camera feeds entirely on the local machine and sends events to Home Assistant — person detected, car in driveway, door left open — without any cloud processing.

Voice input

Whisper (local speech-to-text)

OpenAI's Whisper model runs locally and converts spoken commands to text. The text goes to the language model, which decides what action to take. No audio leaves the house at any point.

Notifications

Local messaging bridge

The system can push notifications to a phone when something is detected or a task completes — a motion alert, a confirmation that the lights turned off, a reminder. The bridge runs on the local network.

What has been done so far

Home Assistant is installed and running on local hardware, controlling several devices in the home. The architecture for the full system — how the components connect, what data flows where, how the language model receives events from Frigate and sends commands to Home Assistant — is designed and documented. Component selection for the local language model hardware and the voice interface is underway.
